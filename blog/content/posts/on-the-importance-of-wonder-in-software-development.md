If you had to sort software engineers by a single attribute to predict their success, which would you pick?

It’s a tough question, but I’d probably pick their sense of wonder.

You really don’t have to be in an engineering discipline very long to realize that some of the best things start with “I wonder if...”.

In that vein, few things in my experience kill an early-career software development interview quicker than the growing suspicion that the candidate isn’t really curious about anything.

Why is it so important to ask good questions when you are being interviewed? It’s not just that companies are vain and like the attention. It’s that if you don’t ask probing questions, it can read as though you don’t wonder about much. That’s not good news, especially early in your career, when you haven’t yet had much experience with the things “on the other side of wonder”.

The world is filled with things to figure out, wonder-filled things, and enigmas wrapped in mysteries. Nurture your curiosity. Sure, curiosity killed the cat, but an incurious cat doesn’t get very far.

As G.K. Chesterton once observed:

> The world will never starve for want of wonders; but only for want of wonder.

That was true before the “Claude era”, and I think it will be true during and after it. Don’t stop wondering. It’s a big key to success.
